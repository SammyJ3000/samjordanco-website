Workflow first, tool second

Practical thinking.
Working tools.

I am not here to sell you AI magic or a giant platform you do not need. I build from the workflow backwards: what happens now, where time gets wasted, and what a useful first version should remove.

My sweet spot is small builds with clear jobs: tools that save time, organise the mess, connect the boring bits, and make the day-to-day work feel less clunky.

Before you overthink it

The stuff people ask before reaching out.

Most good builds start as a slightly embarrassing sentence like, "I keep doing this manually and it is driving me mad."

How long does it take?

Most first versions land in two to three weeks. Smaller jobs are often a few days. You get a fixed date before anything starts rather than an estimate that keeps moving.

What does it cost?

You get a fixed price up front, once I understand the job. Small builds are considerably less than people expect, and if I think the thing is not worth building yet I will tell you before you have spent anything.

What if I only have a rough idea?

That is usually the best place to start. Tell me what is annoying, what you are doing manually, and what you wish happened instead. I will help turn that into a clear first version.

Do I need to know the tech?

No. You need to understand the problem, not the stack. I will handle the technical choices and explain the trade-offs in plain English.

What can you build?

Small web apps, AI tools, internal dashboards, workflow automations, API integrations, and MVPs. The common thread is simple: it needs a clear job and a real person using it.

How does the process work?

We map the workflow, choose the smallest useful version, agree the scope, build in clear stages, and test it against the real job. You see progress early instead of waiting for a big reveal.

What if the idea is too complex?

Then we reduce it. Most complex ideas have a smaller first version hiding inside them. If it genuinely needs a bigger team or is not worth building yet, I will say that.

The easiest next step

Show me the workflow you are tired of doing manually.

Send the messy version. A voice-note-level explanation is enough: what you do now, where it breaks, and what would make the next week easier.
